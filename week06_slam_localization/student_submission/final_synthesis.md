# Final synthesis

Both of my maps were incomplete, showing that while LiDAR is fast, the coverage does not automatically make a complete or reliable map. The robot could estimate wall locations, detect obstacles, and identify some free space, but unknown regions remained. In my runs, moving closer to walls helped reveal boundaries, but an unknown area should not be treated as free space just because no obstacle was detected. SLAM combines motion estimates with LiDAR observations to build the occupancy grid. Errors in the estimated position or orientation can place accurate scans in bad locations. During mapping, I've seen that two rooms appeared to merge even though their layout had originally looked good. This means a mapping or alignment error, although my observations alone cannot confirm the exact issue. Revisiting areas can provide evidence that helps SLAM correct accumulated errors, but my experience showed that the resulting map can also become distorted. I would test shorter, deliberate routes with controlled revisits rather than think that avoiding revisits always improves accuracy. My second route took approximately 65 minutes, yet the map remained incomplete, so additional time did not guarantee better quality. For a later mission, I would trust whichever saved map preserved separate rooms and consistent walls more clearly, rather than whichever covered more space. Neither map supports confident navigation through unknown regions. A changing environment, such as a moved chair, would also require fresh observations and a mapping or obstacle-update process that can revise outdated information.
